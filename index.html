Vered Kurtz-David

Senior Lecturer, Bogen Department of Economics and PPE Program

Principal Investigator — The Jerusalem Neuroeconomics Lab

I study the cognitive and neural mechanisms underlying economic choice. My research combines economic modeling, behavioral experiments, functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), eye-tracking, mouse-tracking, and computational approaches to examine strategic reasoning, individual decision-making, and choice inconsistency.

Research

Current research

My work focuses on several cognitive aspects of economic choice.

Strategic reasoning

How people reason about others, perform iterative calculations, and adapt to strategic environments.

Individual decision-making

Which cognitive resources support risk preferences, and how expectations modify decisions.

Choice inconsistency

Why preferences vary across time and context, and which cognitive and neural mechanisms contribute to inconsistency in choice.
